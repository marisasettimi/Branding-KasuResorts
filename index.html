01 / Brand Overview

Kasu Resorts.

A luxury short-term rental resort brand where great design, real hospitality, and standout locations come together to create stays worth remembering.

i

We believe every guest deserves a place that feels like it was designed just for them. Not generic. Not ordinary. Spaces shaped by intention and warmth.

The Story

Built for travelers who seek more than a place to sleep.

Kasu exists because hospitality should be personal. Every property is designed with intention, managed hands-on, and built around how guests actually want to spend their time.

The brand follows the same logic. Clean but not cold. High-end but not stiff. Everything looks and feels the way the properties do in person.

Typography Guidelines
Do
Use weight variation (200-700) to create clear hierarchy within the single typeface
Keep body text at 400 weight for comfortable reading
Maintain generous line height (1.4-1.8) for breathing room
Use negative letter-spacing (-1px to -2px) on display headlines only
Use EB Garamond exclusively for numbers, statistics, and data displays
Don't
Don't use more than two type sizes on a single card or component
Don't stretch, compress, or outline the letterforms
Don't use EB Garamond for headlines, quotes, body text, buttons, labels, or navigation
Don't set all-caps above 14px. Reserve uppercase for tags and labels
Don't use light weights (200-300) below 16px. They lose legibility

Color Philosophy

The palette references travel without illustrating it.

Blue sky is shorthand for escape. The entire system is tuned to that association: open, light, somewhere else. Every color maps back to the feeling of being away, not the imagery of getting there.

Blue as Destination
Deep Sky and Mid Sky are accent colors used at 8-10% and 3-5% respectively. Blue should feel present but never dominant. Background, not foreground.
Airiness Over Weight
Cloud and white account for over 80% of the palette. The brand should feel light and open. Density and saturation work against the sense of space we're trying to create.
Warm Counterbalance
Sunlit Sand keeps the blues and greys from feeling cold. Use it for tags, highlights, and small warm touches throughout the system.
Dark for Structure
Graphite gives the system hierarchy and legibility. It grounds headlines and UI elements without competing with the lightness of everything around it.

09 / Motion System

Graceful, always.

Motion at Kasu is subtle, useful, and never distracting. If you notice it too much, it's too much.

Motion Principles
01
Purposeful
Every animation has a reason. It guides attention, provides feedback, or creates continuity between states. Never animate for decoration.
02
Subtle
Good motion goes unnoticed. Transitions should be smooth enough that removing them would feel off, but small enough that they never slow anyone down.
03
Consistent
Use the same easing curves and duration scales throughout. Predictable motion builds trust and makes interfaces feel cohesive.
Easing & Duration
Ease Out
The primary easing curve. Elements slow down into their final position. Used for entrances and transitions.
cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)
Ease In-Out
For elements that move between two states. Smooth acceleration and deceleration. Used for toggles, accordions, and state changes.
cubic-bezier(0.45, 0, 0.55, 1)
Quick (150-250ms)
Hover effects, color changes, opacity shifts. These should feel instant but not abrupt. The shortest meaningful animation duration.
Used for micro-interactions
Standard (300-500ms)
Page transitions, card entrances, panel reveals. Enough time to register the motion without slowing the user down.
Used for primary transitions

13 / Product & Data UI

Data has a
visual language.

How Kasu product interfaces present data. Chart palettes, dashboard patterns, metric displays, and the design philosophy that ties guest experience to pixels.

Design Philosophy

Product UI follows the same rules.

The product interface is not a separate brand. It is the brand at its most functional. The same restraint, the same warmth, the same precision.

01
Data First
Every screen starts with the number that matters most. Layout, hierarchy, and whitespace exist to serve that number.
02
Progressive Disclosure
Show the headline metric. Let users drill down. Information density is earned through interaction, not forced on arrival.
03
Calm Precision
The product should feel calm and useful, not like a stock ticker. Animations are subtle, color is strategic, and the interface stays out of the way.
Chart Styling

Every chart speaks the same dialect.

Charts are data presentations, not decorations. No gradients, no 3D effects, no chart junk. Every pixel of ink should encode data or provide structure.

Sparkline
Blue stroke with subtle gradient fill. Pulsing endpoint dot. For KPI cards.
